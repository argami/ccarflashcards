Anatomy of a card

It is not a quiz. It is the reason you would fail.

Every card reproduces the real exam format — a production scenario, one question, four plausible options (five, two or three correct, on the multiple-response items) — then opens up in layers down to the source text.

01

Scenario, not definition

A real system, something breaking, a decision pending. Roughly 56 words of context on average.

02

Why each distractor fails

All three wrong options get their own explanation. Learning why something looks reasonable is what separates 700 from 800.

03

Trap tip

Each card is tagged with one of eight classic traps (iteration cap, context inheritance, prompt instead of code…) and how to spot it in five seconds.

04

Verbatim quote, not a summary

652 quotes of 15 to 40 words lifted literally from the official exam guide and the 30 lessons. The build rejects any paraphrase.

05

Genuinely bilingual

Question and options in English, like the exam. Explanation in Spanish and English, with measured parity: 327,329 versus 317,681 characters.

06

Real questions included

50 questions from a real exam bank, enriched without touching the wording or the correct answer, marked as a reference set.

How to use it

Two paths, same content

In the browser

  1. Download ccarflashcards.html and double-click it.
  2. Answer with the keyboard (1–4, or 1–5 on multiple-response cards) or the mouse. Space for the next card.
  3. Your progress stays in that browser. On another machine you start from zero.

In Anki

  1. Install Anki (desktop or AnkiDroid, both free) and open File → Import.
  2. Pick the downloaded .apkg. The CCAR-F deck appears with five sub-decks.
  3. Study a little every day. Anki decides what comes back and when; you just answer.

Quick answers

Do I need an account or a connection?

No. The HTML is one self-contained file: it calls no server, loads no external fonts and works on a plane. Only AnkiWeb asks for an account, and only if you want to sync.

Does my progress survive if I delete the file?

Progress lives in the browser, not in the file. Deleting the HTML does not delete it; opening it in another browser does start empty. In Anki your progress is in your collection.

Where do the quotes come from?

From the official exam guide (CCAR-F, version 1.0) and the 30 public lessons in the syllabus. Every quote is a literal 15-to-40-word fragment and the validation script confirms it exists verbatim before the build.

Is this official Anthropic material?

No. It is study material made by someone preparing for the exam. The questions are not the real exam's, although the format and difficulty imitate it.
